Length, division, and the slow‑music trick

Each track's pattern has its own LENGTH (1 to 64 steps) and its own DIV, how much musical time one step lasts: from 1/32 of a bar up to one or even two whole bars per step. Both live on the SEQ hold (KNOB 4 and KNOB 2). One honest mechanic first: changing DIV never moves notes between steps; every step simply lasts longer or shorter, so flipping a recorded 1/16 pattern to 1BAR just stretches the whole take sixteen‑fold. Slow music is best built slow:

Build a slow chord bed from a clean start

before you clear

This replaces the pad's pattern, so park the loop somewhere safe first: hold SAVE: among keys 5–8, a dark key is an empty section, and pressing a dark one saves the whole loop there without touching anything else (lit keys already hold music, so leave those alone; Lesson 10 explains sections). If all four are lit, or you would rather not touch sections yet, postpone this exercise until after Lesson 10; nothing else depends on it.

  1. Select the pad, track 1, and clear its pattern: hold REC about two seconds until the ring fills. (EDIT+OCT− right now brings it straight back.)

  2. Hold SEQ: turn KNOB 4 (LENGTH) to 4 and KNOB 2 (DIV) to 1BAR. Four steps, each lasting a whole bar.

  3. Give this track chords too: CHORD is per‑track (Lesson 5 set it on track 2 only), so hold SEL and turn KNOB 1 to TRIAD here; key and scale are shared by all synth tracks and already right.

  4. Write a known chord onto each step (an empty step takes the chord you played last): play key 5 (the home chord, I), then SEQ+key 1 to put it on step 1; play key 10 (VI), then SEQ+key 2 for step 2; play key 8 (IV), then SEQ+key 3 for step 3; play key 9 (V), then SEQ+key 4 for step 4.

  5. PLAY. The pad changes chord once a bar (how much of each bar it rings is the PATTERN page's GATE; your pad's long release does the rest) while the drums tick in 1/16 underneath. For glacial: DIV 2BAR, one chord every two bars.

Polymeter, free of charge

Length belongs to a whole track (all sixteen drum sounds share track 4's one pattern and length), so the trick lives between tracks: set this chord bed's LENGTH to 3 and a three‑chord round turns against the four‑bar drums, realigning every twelve bars. Set it back to 4 when done; the fourth chord returns, since shortening hides far steps rather than erasing them.

you should now be hearing

A beat you can see and edit cell by cell, and a chord bed you placed by hand that changes on a one‑bar clock over drums ticking in 1/16: two timescales in one loop.

try this

On the grid, demote every hat except the ones on the beat to ghost with KNOB 4, and push one unexpected hat to hard. Tiny level moves like this are where grooves actually live.

made a mess?

A step you just set: tap its key again (SEQ held, or on the grid) and it is gone; EDIT+OCT− undoes the last change (one level, so after further edits the road back to the old pad pass is to record it again). A silent synth pattern? Check DIV: at 1BAR it crawls, and may not have reached its next step yet.
